Dihexa: Synaptogenesis, HGF/c-Met & the Limits of the Evidence

Dihexa is promoted online as an unusually powerful cognitive and neuroregenerative compound. The underlying scientific record is far less certain: intriguing preclinical findings coexist with zero human trials and serious integrity problems affecting foundational papers.

The short version: Dihexa is an angiotensin-IV-derived peptidomimetic investigated for effects on learning, dendritic spine formation and synaptic connectivity. Its widely cited HGF/c-Met mechanism is scientifically interesting—but the principal 2014 mechanism paper was formally retracted in 2025, while the 2013 paper describing Dihexa's behavioral and pharmacologic properties remains under a Notice of Concern.

What Is Dihexa?

Dihexa, also known as PNB-0408, is N-hexanoyl-Tyr-Ile-(6)-aminohexanoic amide, a synthetic peptidomimetic developed from research into angiotensin IV and related molecules involved in learning and memory.

The original research program attempted to create metabolically stable compounds capable of reaching the brain while preserving the procognitive effects reported for angiotensin-IV analogues. A 2013 animal study reported that Dihexa reached multiple brain regions, improved performance in rodent memory models and increased dendritic spine formation.

Evidence-quality alert: that 2013 study received a formal Notice of Concern in 2021. It has not been retracted as of October 2026, but its integrity flag materially lowers confidence in claims that depend on it.

The Proposed HGF/c-Met Connection

The best-known explanation for Dihexa's effects has been potentiation of hepatocyte growth factor (HGF) signaling through the c-Met receptor. HGF/c-Met biology participates in cell survival, growth, migration and tissue repair, while neuronal research has connected the pathway with dendritic development and synaptic plasticity.

Dihexa→ HGF signaling→ c-Met→ PI3K / AKT and related signaling→ Synaptic / neuronal effects

A highly cited 2014 paper reported direct high-affinity interaction with HGF, enhancement of HGF-dependent c-Met activation, and increased hippocampal spinogenesis and synaptogenesis. Those results became the foundation for much of the modern Dihexa narrative.

Critical update: the 2014 HGF/c-Met mechanism paper was formally retracted in April 2025. The retraction followed an institutional investigation and means its experimental findings should not be treated as established evidence. Any description of HGF/c-Met as Dihexa's mechanism should therefore be labeled proposed, not proven.

Synaptogenesis: Why Dihexa Became Famous

Dihexa attracted attention because early studies reported increased dendritic spine formation and functional synaptic connectivity. In neuroscience, this is a much more interesting claim than simply increasing alertness: synaptogenesis refers to the formation of connections through which neurons communicate.

Early rodent experiments also reported improved spatial learning in pharmacologically impaired and aged animals. These findings helped create Dihexa's reputation as a potential neuroregenerative or cognition-enhancing molecule.

But the integrity problems affecting the originating literature substantially change how those claims should now be presented. They are historical research findings requiring independent confirmation—not settled facts about the compound.

Alzheimer's Disease Models

Independent researchers have continued to examine Dihexa. A 2021 APP/PS1 mouse study reported improved performance in cognitive tasks and changes in Alzheimer's-associated pathology, neuroinflammation and PI3K/AKT signaling.

This provides evidence outside the earliest Dihexa papers that the compound can produce measurable biological effects in an Alzheimer's mouse model. It still does not establish efficacy in people, and an Alzheimer's mouse model cannot reproduce the full complexity of human Alzheimer's disease.

Is Dihexa a “10 Million Times Stronger BDNF”?

This is one of the most repeated claims surrounding Dihexa online—and one of the poorest ways to describe the evidence.

A potency comparison between an experimental peptidomimetic and brain-derived neurotrophic factor can depend entirely on the assay, endpoint and concentration being compared. More importantly, the dramatic “millions of times stronger” formulation cannot be treated as evidence that Dihexa produces millions of times more neuroplasticity, memory improvement or clinical benefit.

ProtokolX does not use this claim as a meaningful measure of Dihexa's effectiveness.

The Human Evidence Problem

The largest limitation is straightforward: Dihexa itself has no published human clinical evidence.

As of October 2026, searches of the published and registered evidence do not identify a completed human pharmacokinetic, dose-ranging, safety or efficacy trial of Dihexa itself. There is therefore no validated human therapeutic dose, no established clinical benefit and no adequately characterized human adverse-effect profile.

Related compounds are not Dihexa: clinical findings from other HGF/MET modulators cannot simply be transferred to Dihexa. Similar pathway hypotheses do not make two molecules pharmacologically interchangeable.

The c-Met Safety Question

The HGF/c-Met pathway deserves particular caution because c-Met is a receptor tyrosine kinase with powerful growth and survival functions. Dysregulated MET signaling is also implicated in multiple cancers.

That does not establish that Dihexa causes cancer. There are no human Dihexa data capable of answering that question.

It does mean that long-term manipulation of a growth-associated pathway requires serious toxicology and clinical safety work. For Dihexa, those data are notably absent. The appropriate conclusion is uncertainty—not reassurance and not an unsupported claim of harm.

What Dihexa Is — and What It Isn't

What can reasonably be said

  • Dihexa is an experimental angiotensin-IV-derived peptidomimetic.
  • Rodent research has reported effects on memory and synaptic biology.
  • HGF/c-Met signaling has been proposed as a central mechanism.
  • An independent 2021 mouse study reported cognitive and signaling effects in an APP/PS1 Alzheimer's model.
  • The compound remains scientifically interesting as a neuroplasticity research tool.

What the evidence does not establish

  • No proven cognitive enhancement in humans.
  • No established treatment for Alzheimer's, Parkinson's, TBI or other neurological disease.
  • No validated human dose or dosing schedule.
  • No adequate human short- or long-term safety profile.
  • The foundational HGF/c-Met mechanism cannot be treated as settled after the 2025 retraction.

The Retraction Problem Matters

Scientific retractions are not a minor footnote. When a foundational mechanism paper is retracted, later articles and marketing claims that rely on that paper need to be reevaluated.

In Dihexa's case, the 2014 paper that strongly connected the compound's procognitive and synaptogenic activity to HGF/c-Met was retracted in 2025. Related work from the originating research program also received formal integrity actions, while the important 2013 Dihexa paper remains under a Notice of Concern.

This does not prove that every reported effect of Dihexa is false. It means confidence should be downgraded until key findings are independently reproduced.

ProtokolX Assessment

Dihexa is a perfect example of why “Verify then Trust” matters.

The hypothesis is compelling: influence a neurotrophic signaling system, promote synaptic connectivity and potentially improve neuronal resilience. Preclinical observations give researchers reasons to keep asking questions.

But the evidence hierarchy matters more than the excitement surrounding the mechanism. Dihexa has no established human efficacy, no meaningful human safety database, and part of the literature supporting its best-known mechanism has been retracted.

Dihexa should therefore be viewed as a highly experimental neuroplasticity research compound—not an established cognitive enhancer.
